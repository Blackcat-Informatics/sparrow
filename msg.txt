Default archive segments to 10,000 entries and report every halved prepare (#321, #322)

Cause: a 50,000-entry prepare took 31.8 s on a quiet host, but on a loaded host
(load average 79 on 32 cores) the 60 s RDF read lease ended at 63 s. The retry
at 25,000 entries took 53 s, about 2.1 ms per entry loaded. Store::archive_prepare
then halved the range and dropped the encode error that caused it, so neither the
operator nor the worker could tell that a segment was shorter than selected.

Change:
- ARCHIVE_SEGMENT_RECORDS is 10,000 (about 21 s loaded, 6.4 s quiet, as measured
  before this change). The reference docs state the new default.
- Every halving emits a structured warning (code, message, entries, new cap,
  elapsed_ms) and is counted by Store::archive_halvings.
- PreparedSegment carries an additive Shortened (selected, kept, first causing
  code), absent when the range was not halved. The archive worker logs it, writes
  an operator line and keeps a running count in its in-process Flight state.
  No wire or stored-format change: the daemon status does not carry the count,
  because that needs a Cap'n Proto field.
- architecture.md section 4.4 now says GTS segments are written through PurRDF's
  GTS writer and that archive reads give way to waiting writers and hold the single
  RDF read slot. archive-format.md's phase paragraph states that encoding, the
  worker and the offline reader are implemented and that the worker is not running
  on the live daemon.

Evidence: sparrow-core test archive_segment (new test
a_prepare_halved_by_an_encode_failure_reports_the_shortening_and_counts_it holds
the RDF read slot through the first attempt, so the first encode really fails
with RESOURCE_LIMIT, and frees it at the halving's warning); sparrow-server
library tests matching archive.

Not verified: the 10,000-entry timings were not remeasured here; behaviour on the
live daemon; the full gate beyond the commit hook.
