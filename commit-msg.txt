Renew the archive read's lease while it progresses; verify each identity once

A 10,000-record segment outlasted the archive read's fixed 60-second
lease under the unit's CPU cap, so prepares were halved and their work
thrown away (#328).

Profile (400 and 2,000 katamari-shaped records, release): the scoped
read already walks one cursor per owner graph. The cost was identity
verification: every index row checked the forward and reverse
dictionary rows of all four of its terms, in both the named pass and
the default-union pass, about 16 of the 19 requests per stored row.
A view now verifies each dictionary identity once, the first time a row
names it, and charges the entry to its live bytes; the snapshot cannot
change, so later rows skip both point reads. Requests fall to 2.9 per
row for log records and 4.8 for an 800-key JSON record, and the
archive's row allowance drops from 42 to 10 requests to match.

The archive slot's read renews its lease on every admitted read, so it
ends only after 60 seconds without progress. Query reads keep their
fixed lease. A stalled prepare still ends with RESULT_EXPIRED, is
halved and reports the shortening.

Rehearsal, 50,000 records under CPUQuota=100% and MemoryHigh=2G to
NFS: 6 segments, 0 halvings, prepare 145 s in total (was 2,098 s),
worst segment 33 s for 10,000 records (was 204 s).
